ABC Inc.

Spatial UI for car booking — see it before you book it.

A mobile car booking experience that adds AR in-context viewing and a VR showroom, so groups booking SUVs and buses can judge size and fit instead of guessing from static photos.

The Problem

Static listings, hesitant bookings.

ABC Inc., a local car booking service, runs a website of static images and text-heavy listings. Users get little confidence in a vehicle's size, fit or condition, which shows up as high bounce rates and low engagement — especially for group bookings of SUVs and buses where space and configuration decide the booking.

The bet

Replace uncertainty with direct evaluation. AR puts the vehicle in the user's real surroundings; a VR showroom lets them walk around it. Both aim to cut hesitation and lift booking conversion.

PERSONA 01

Family Traveler

Booking an SUV for a weekend trip. Cares about seating and luggage space.

PERSONA 02

Corporate Group Coordinator

Booking a bus for an offsite. Cares about capacity and comparing options quickly.

DESIGN GOAL

Keep it consistent

A reusable component system (buttons, inputs, vehicle cards, bottom nav) keeps the AR/VR screens visually tied to the rest of the app.

User Flow

One path, two immersive branches.

Home / Search→Listing→Detail→View in AR/Explore VR Showroom→Customize→Booking
High-Fidelity Screens

Six screens, 375 × 812.

All six are linked into one clickable Figma prototype, covering Home through Booking including both the AR and VR branches.

AR / VR Prototypes

From flat frames to real space.

A 3D showroom was built in Spline, then the VR dashboard was tested at human scale in ShapesXR.

Spline · 3D showroom

A car model on a lit floor plane, framed at a 3/4 showroom hero angle. Clicking the car triggers a smooth full-turn rotation (States + Transition) so users can inspect every angle.

ShapesXR · human scale

The VR Showroom frame was exported via the Figma plugin and placed at eye level, directly in front of a human-scale avatar, at a comfortable arm's-length-plus distance. This confirmed the spatial layout reads correctly.

Eye level · centered

The vehicle stage is the primary focal point.

Just below · in reach

The color/trim panel sits within easy reach.

Closest to user

Navigation sits nearest, since switching vehicles is the most frequent action.

Scope call: a color-swap interaction in Spline was attempted but de-scoped after hitting per-mesh material limits. The rotate interaction alone demonstrates the showroom concept within the timeline.
Usability Review

Where the flow might trip people.

An expert heuristic walkthrough of every screen. Each is clear once reached; the risk is finding the way in.

ScreenObservationPossible fix
HomeThe "Explore in 3D" banner may be missed because the search card dominates first attention.Reinforce AR/VR discovery at Detail.
ListingThe icon-only AR badge may not say "viewable in AR".Add a text label.
DetailTwo similarly styled outlined buttons may cause hesitation.Differentiate the two CTAs.
AR ViewRotate / scale / color icons have no labels and assume AR familiarity.Add text labels.
VR ShowroomThe subtle "2 of 8" counter may leave users unaware more vehicles exist.Strengthen the affordance.
BookingNo significant friction, as intended.None.
Overall finding: the biggest risk is discoverability of the AR/VR entry points. They rely on small icons rather than explicit, labeled invitations.
Next: user testing. Planned with 2–3 participants on the Figma prototype. Tasks: find and view a car in AR unaided; explore the showroom from different angles; complete a booking. Follow-ups: "What was confusing?" and "What did you expect to happen that didn't?"

Conclusion

Replacing uncertainty with experience.

By letting users place a vehicle in their own environment and explore a 3D showroom at human scale, the platform swaps doubt for direct evaluation. That should reduce hesitation and improve conversion for the group vehicles, SUVs and buses, where size and fit matter most.
