Malligai

Sustainable South Indian fashion, made measurable.

Malligai is a mobile marketplace for sustainable South Indian fashion — salwar suits, kurtas, and ethnic wear crafted from organic, ethically sourced cotton. The design pairs a warm, trust-building palette with quantified sustainability signals — Eco Score, Eco Points, and water-saved counters — so "sustainable" becomes a number you can see, not just a claim on a label.

PROJECT OVERVIEW

Sustainability you can see.

Sustainable fashion apps tend to lean on messaging — words like "eco-friendly" that ask for trust without earning it. Malligai's brand needed to represent a South Indian craft culture and build genuine calm, personal trust with shoppers, so the design surfaces sustainability as data: an Eco Score on every product, an Eco Points balance that grows with every purchase, and a running tally of water saved and trees planted.

Problem
Sustainability claims felt abstract, so they weren't influencing the purchase decision.
Goal
Make eco-conscious choices visible, quantified, and rewarding at every step.
Outcome
A 9-screen product with a documented design system, tested with 12 real participants.
Constraint
Solo project — research, UI, and the full developer handoff scoped independently.
malligai-overview.png
THE CHALLENGE

Green branding is easy. Proving the green claim, inside a checkout flow that still has to feel effortless, is the harder design problem.

01
Trust without proof
"Sustainable" is a word every fashion brand uses. The interface needed a way to make the claim verifiable at a glance — without turning every product card into a spec sheet.
02
One-time virtue, not a habit
A single sustainable purchase doesn't build a habit. The product needed a reason for shoppers to come back — and to feel that returning was itself part of the impact.
03
A checkout with more to track
Beyond size, quantity, and payment, checkout also had to surface an Eco Discount and a water-saved impact banner — without the extra information adding friction to placing the order.

RESEARCH

Listening before designing.

Before any wireframe, I mapped how people actually evaluate a sustainable fashion purchase — what earns trust, what feels like greenwashing, and where existing eco-commerce apps fall back on vague messaging instead of evidence.

User Interviews
One-on-one conversations with prospective shoppers to surface what "sustainable" needed to mean for them to actually change a purchase decision.
Competitive Analysis
Audited existing sustainable and ethnic-wear marketplaces to see where eco-messaging built trust — and where it read as decoration.
Affinity Mapping
Clustered interview findings in FigJam to isolate the recurring theme: people wanted numbers, not adjectives.
Persona & Journey Mapping
Synthesised findings into a primary persona and a browse-to-checkout journey map, flagging where impact data could be shown without slowing the flow.
KEY INSIGHTS

What the research kept saying.

"
People wanted proof, not promises.
A percentage on the product page — an Eco Score — read as more credible than any amount of "eco-friendly" copy, because it looked measured rather than marketed.
"
People needed a reason to come back, not just to convert once.
Framing sustainable purchases as progress toward something — Eco Points, a Seed → Bloom → Forest tier — turned a one-off decision into an ongoing relationship with the brand.
"
People wanted the impact to feel personal, not abstract.
"320L of water saved" landed harder than a sustainability badge — a concrete, individual number tied to their own order, not the brand's marketing claim.
HOW MIGHT WE
Make sustainability feel measurable and rewarding, instead of aspirational and easy to doubt?
INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Five ways through the product.

Rather than one linear path, Malligai's IA supports five distinct flows — because a shopper deciding on a single item and a shopper checking their Eco Points balance are doing fundamentally different things.

Browse & Purchase
Home→Shop→Product Detail→Add to Cart→My Cart→Checkout→Order Confirmation
Quick Buy
Home / Shop→Product Detail→Buy Now→Checkout→Order Confirmation
Wishlist
Any Product Card→Tap Heart→Wishlist Tab→Product Detail→Add to Cart
Rewards
Home (Eco Points widget)→Rewards Tab→View Levels & Rewards→Redeem
Profile & Settings
Profile Tab→Orders / Addresses / Payment / Reward History / Settings
DESIGN SYSTEM

From concept palette to handoff tokens.

Green carries the brand's South Indian identity and its sustainability promise — it reads as calm, and it builds a personal, trustworthy feel. The palette went through a real revision: an early warmer, lighter exploration was deepened for the final handoff, giving headers and CTAs stronger contrast and more authority.

HIGH FIDELITY SCREENS

Nine screens, one promise.

screen-home.png
01 — Home
The Eco Points widget, up front

A personalised greeting, hero banner, category grid, and trending products sit above an Eco Points teaser — the loyalty mechanic is visible from the very first screen, not buried in a settings menu.

screen-pdp.png
02 — Product Detail
Eco Score, next to the price

An Eco Score badge sits directly beside the price and description — the same decision point where a shopper is weighing cost against value, sustainability included.

screen-cart.png
03 — My Cart
The Eco Discount, made visible

A price breakdown separates the Eco Discount from delivery charges, and a sustainability banner confirms water saved — the impact is quantified before the shopper even reaches checkout.

screen-checkout.png
04 — Checkout
Address, payment, and price — in order

Delivery address and payment method (UPI pre-selected) are separated into their own cards above the price summary — the exact structure later put under a microscope in usability testing.

screen-rewards.png
05 — Rewards
Seed, Bloom, Forest

A three-tier progress system turns Eco Points into a visible journey, paired with a real impact table — trees planted, water saved, carbon reduced — tying gamification back to an actual outcome.

screen-confirmation.png
06 — Order Confirmation
The only screen without a nav bar

A deliberate exception to the system — the bottom nav is hidden here, keeping full focus on the confirmation and the Eco Points just earned, with no back navigation once an order is placed.

Plus Shop / Collection, Wishlist, and Profile — all nine screens are fully specified in the design handoff document.

USABILITY TESTING

Testing the checkout flow.

I ran a moderated study with 12 participants through Maze, focused on the address, payment, and price breakdown steps — the flow most likely to determine whether a shopper who trusted the Eco Score actually completed the purchase.

12
Participants
100%
Task success rate
3

↑
Misclick rate flagged
The paradox: every participant completed checkout successfully — but misclick tracking revealed a surprisingly high rate of accidental taps, particularly around the size dropdown and address radio buttons. Success masked friction that a task-completion metric alone would have missed.
AreaBeforeAfter

REFLECTION

Looking back.

What I learned
Task success alone can hide real friction. The misclick data around the address and size controls was the more honest signal, and it changed how I read every usability report since.
What I'd improve
The developer handoff checklist itself flags the gaps: empty states for Wishlist and Cart, error states for payment failure and out-of-stock, and the address form were all left "to be designed." Those are next.
Future scope
Extending the Eco Score pattern beyond the product page — into order history and the Rewards tab — so the impact data keeps compounding visibly, not just accumulating quietly in the background.
