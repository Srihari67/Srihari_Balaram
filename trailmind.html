01 — Overview

Planning a trip shouldn't need ten tabs.

Sarah, 34, a marketing manager from Chicago, loves travelling but jumps between disconnected apps to match plans to her budget. The result: stress, lost free time, and dulled excitement. Every screen was checked against a problem Sarah actually has.

03 — Research

People don't need ideas. They need to close decisions.

A 33-response survey found patterns at scale; two interviews explained the why. Both pointed to the same gap: narrowing down to a stay and itinerary they can commit to.

19/33abandoned a trip over planning effort
4.2/5importance of budget
25/33track itineraries in a bare Notes app
28/33plan primarily on their phone
01

Competitive gap

Across 11 products, none showed a clear budget-fit signal. AI planners are neutral but weak on booking; OTAs are the reverse. TrailMind sits in the open space between.

02

Time sink

"Building the itinerary" (14) edged out "Comparing prices" (12) as the biggest pain point.

03

Conditional trust

Interviewees stop trusting an AI plan the moment it feels rushed, unrealistic or off-budget — so every AI block stays editable.

04 — Structure & flows

Two taps to what matters.

A shallow, tab-anchored structure: Home, Explore, My Trips (emphasised as the core value), Alerts and Profile.

01

Onboarding

Preference setup is mandatory before first Home, so recommendations feel earned rather than generic.

02

Discovery

A budget-fit indicator sits on the card itself, before the detail page opens.

03

Itinerary

The AI draft is a starting point: reorder, swap, lock a day.

04

Booking

Price breakdown comes before payment method, so there's no surprise total.

05

Live updates

Alerts deep-link to the affected itinerary day, so changes are seen in context.

06 — Usability testing

The flow I trusted worked. The one I ignored didn't.

9participants, 12 test blocks (Maze)
3.9/5average design rating
100%best single-task success (Kyoto booking)
56%completed both tasks

Booking a trip: validated

75% and 100% success across variants. A ~61% misclick rate says the primary CTA needs more visual weight on a couple of screens.

Past trip summary: broken

0% and 28.6% success. The Past tab showed only a cover, name and rating, with no entry point to what was booked, spent or visited.

07 — Iterations

Gaps found while building.

01

Flight & hotel detail

Added detail and swap screens so users see exactly what they're committing to before checkout.

02

Trip summary

A single review screen between the itinerary and booking summary.

03

Home redundancy

Alerts appeared in three places. The header icon became Saved, and quick actions became New Trip and Continue Itinerary.

04

Dead ends removed

Built Add Activity, Edit Profile and Change Password; added search to the filter sheet.

08 — Next steps

What I'd do next.

Fix summary discoverability

Add "View summary" to every Past trip card; rebuild around photos, spend, places and rating.

Strengthen booking CTAs

Raise visual dominance to cut misclicks.
